The trip in brief

Five days from Den Bosch through Roman Trier and Strasbourg to the Alsatian wine villages, and back. Four nights across three bases, with the longest leg along the way being three hours. The only heavy stretch is the drive home on Sunday, 5.5 hours from Colmar, so that morning starts early.

The plan deliberately puts the wine tasting on Friday 14 August rather than Saturday. Saturday is Assumption Day, a national holiday in France, when many wine estates and shops close. That leaves Saturday free for Colmar, Eguisheim and the Route des Vins, which stay open and lively.

There are two versions of the schedule, switchable below: five days with a night in Trier, or four days with Trier as a lunch stop on the way south. The route, the mileage and the total driving time are identical. What the shorter version costs is an evening and a morning in Trier, and it turns day one into a five-and-a-half-hour drive.

It works with hotels or with a camper. The route and the timings are identical either way, only the overnight stops change. The camper version is set out further down.

Day by day

Four nights, three bases. The gentlest version. Trier gets a proper evening and an unhurried morning, and no single day is heavy except the drive home.
Wed 12 Aug

Den Bosch → Trier

305 km · ±3 h 10

An easy transfer day. You arrive early enough to walk into town and eat properly.

  • 14:30Leave Vughterstraat. Route via Eindhoven, Aachen and Luxembourg. Fill up in Luxembourg, it saves a lot.
  • 16:30Coffee stop somewhere near the Luxembourg border.
  • 18:00Arrive in Trier. Check in, drop the bags.
  • 19:00Evening walk to the Hauptmarkt and Porta Nigra, then dinner on a terrace by the square. A Moselle Riesling to go with it.
Sleep: in Trier itself (everything walkable, easiest for a car-free evening), or more romantically along the Moselle in Trittenheim, Piesport or Neumagen-Dhron, 25 to 35 minutes away.
Thu 13 Aug

Trier → Strasbourg

230 km · ±2 h 20

Roman Trier in the morning, drive on after lunch. You are in Strasbourg well before aperitif hour.

  • 09:30Porta Nigra (worth going inside), then the Dom and the Konstantinbasilika.
  • 11:30Kaiserthermen or the amphitheatre, depending on how much Roman you can take.
  • 12:30Lunch in the old town.
  • 14:00Drive to Strasbourg via Saarbrücken. Park in a garage or a P+R, not in the centre.
  • 16:30Check in, then out into the city. Cathedral, astronomical clock, Petite France.
  • 19:30Dinner at a winstub with a bottle of Alsatian. Plenty of them in and around Petite France.
Sleep: central Strasbourg, around the Grande Île or Petite France. Check in advance whether the hotel sorts out parking.
Fri 14 Aug

Strasbourg → Wettolsheim

75 km · ±1 h

Wine day. Deliberately on Friday, because tomorrow is a public holiday and the estate is likely to be closed.

  • 09:00Slow morning in Strasbourg. Market, coffee, maybe a boat trip on the Ill.
  • 11:30Head south. Take the A35, or if you have time, the Route des Vins past Obernai and Riquewihr.
  • 13:00Lunch on the way, in Kaysersberg or Riquewihr for instance.
  • 15:00Tasting in Wettolsheim. By appointment, see below.
  • 17:00Check in in Wettolsheim, Eguisheim or Colmar.
  • 19:30Dinner in Colmar or Eguisheim. Eguisheim is five minutes away and looks like a fairy tale after dark.
Sleep: two nights in the same place, which saves hauling luggage around. Wettolsheim is quiet, Eguisheim is the prettiest village, Colmar has the most going on.
Sat 15 Aug

Alsace, free day

50 to 90 km locally

Public holiday in France. Shops and many estates closed, but villages, terraces and restaurants open and busy. A good day for wandering rather than planning.

  • 09:30Colmar in the morning, while it is still calm. Petite Venise and the Unterlinden.
  • 12:30Lunch on a terrace.
  • 14:30Route des Vins heading north: Eguisheim, Turckheim, Kaysersberg, Riquewihr.
  • 16:00Alternative: Château du Haut-Kœnigsbourg (40 min) or a walk through the vineyards up to the Grand Cru Steingrubler, right behind Wettolsheim.
  • 19:30Last evening in Colmar or Eguisheim. Busy, it being a holiday, so eat a little earlier or a little later.
Sleep: same address as last night. Pack up this evening.
Sun 16 Aug

Wettolsheim → Den Bosch

545 km · ±5 h 30

The one long day. Leaving early is the difference between home at four and home at seven.

  • 09:00Breakfast and check out.
  • 09:45Depart. Route via Strasbourg, Karlsruhe, Cologne. Or via Metz and Luxembourg if you want one more cheap tank.
  • 12:30Lunch stop, roughly halfway.
  • 15:30Home. Well before the end of the afternoon.
Note: mid-August is when France swaps its holidaymakers over, and the Sunday after 15 August can be busy heading north. Leave before ten.

What goes wrong if you do nothing

Saturday 15 August is a national holiday in France

Assumption Day. Supermarkets, shops and a fair share of wine estates are closed, while restaurants and villages are extra busy. That is why the tasting sits on Friday and not Saturday.

Domaine Albert Mann is out: summer closure

Wettolsheim's best-known estate is closed from 30 July through 17 August. Exactly your week. Do not bother emailing, pick an alternative.

Strasbourg has a low-emission zone (ZFE)

You need a Crit'Air sticker to drive into the centre, Dutch plates included. It costs a few euros but delivery easily takes one to two weeks, so order it now rather than the week itself. Use the official French site only, plenty of resellers charge double.

Wine tasting in Wettolsheim

Book now, not on the day

In August the good Alsatian estates receive almost exclusively by appointment. An email this week is enough. Mention the date, the time, how many of you there are, and that you are coming from the Netherlands.

First choice · Wettolsheim

Domaine Barmès-Buecher

Biodynamic since 1998, 18 hectares, nine grape varieties. The tasting room sits in a converted barn. By appointment only.

Domaine Albert Mann

A top estate, but closed from 30 July through 17 August. Save it for next time.

Backup · Eguisheim, 5 min

Emile Beyer or Paul Ginglinger

Eguisheim sits next to Wettolsheim and has caveaux that stay open without an appointment in high season. Useful as a plan B, or simply as a second tasting.

Walk · Wettolsheim

Grand Cru Steingrubler

The vineyard path starts directly behind the village. An hour uphill, views across the Rhine plain and, on a clear day, all the way to the Black Forest.

If you take the camper instead

Same route, easier logistics

Nothing about the day-by-day plan changes. You drop three hotel bookings and pick up three overnight spots, all of which sit within walking or tram distance of what you came to see. Add roughly ten to fifteen percent to the driving times, and keep in mind that the Route des Vins villages have narrow streets, so park outside and walk in.

Wed 12 Aug · Trier

Reisemobilpark Treviris

Right on the Moselle, about fifteen minutes on foot across the bridge into the old town. Full services, open year round, no reservation needed. The obvious choice for a car-free evening.

Thu 13 Aug · Strasbourg

Camping de Strasbourg, Montagne Verte

Four-star site west of the centre with a bus and tram connection straight into town, so you can have that bottle of Alsatian without thinking about it. Book this one, August fills up.

Alternative: the aire at the Deux Rives youth hostel, closer in but plainer.

Fri 14 & Sat 15 Aug · Alsace

Aire d'Eguisheim, Grand Rue

Behind the town hall, on the edge of arguably the prettiest village in Alsace, five minutes from Wettolsheim. Walk into the village for dinner. Popular in August, so arrive in the early afternoon.

Alternative: Camping de l'Ill in Colmar, with a bus into the centre.

Worth setting up · All of France

France Passion

A membership scheme that lets you park overnight for free at participating farms and wine estates, and Alsace is well covered. No facilities, one night, no obligation to buy (though obviously you will). Order the guide well before you leave, it arrives by post.

The real camper advantage

A tasting where you have to drive afterwards is a tasting where someone spits. If you can park for the night at or near the estate, Friday afternoon becomes a genuinely different afternoon. Mention it when you email Barmès-Buecher, or line up a France Passion address nearby as the backup.

The Crit'Air sticker still applies

Campers are not exempt from Strasbourg's low-emission zone. And check your vehicle's Crit'Air class before you order: older diesel campers can fall into a category that is restricted rather than merely stickered. Worth five minutes now instead of a conversation at a checkpoint later.

Alternatives if you want less driving

Skip or shorten Strasbourg

If you would rather stay put longer, cut Strasbourg down to a long afternoon and evening and skip the overnight there. That gives you: Wednesday and Thursday on the Moselle, Friday afternoon in Strasbourg as a stop along the way, and three nights in Alsace. Same amount of driving, but you unpack twice instead of three times.

Or more: add the Vosges

On Saturday, the Route des Crêtes starting from Munster (20 minutes from Wettolsheim) is a beautiful mountain drive with views over the whole of Alsace. Allow half a day and a fair number of hairpins.
