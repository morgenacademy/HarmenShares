Where you are now

The Moselle is done: Trier on Wednesday evening, the Mosel bank on Thursday morning, and Carl Loewen in Leiwen at three. What is left is Alsace and the drive home.

Friday is the transfer, down the Luxembourg bank with fuel at Wasserbillig, check in at Eguisheim and Wolfberger at four. Saturday is Colmar in the morning and Schoepfer-Muller at four. Sunday is 545 km home in the rain.

The camper is a Hymer Free 602 on a Fiat Ducato, 5.99 m long and 2.60 m tall. That length clears the 6.50 m limit some sites impose, and the height puts you in French toll class 2.

Day by day

Fri 14 Aug

Schweich → Eguisheim

313 km · ±3 h 35

37°C, the hottest day of the trip. Straight through, no Strasbourg: the van is air-conditioned, and arriving at one means the worst of the afternoon is spent sitting still in the shade.

  • 08:45Break camp and go down the Luxembourg bank. Wasserbillig, Remich, Schengen, then back into Germany at Perl for Saarbrücken and the A4. About 35 km further than the direct route and a good deal prettier. Leaving at ten instead lands you there at ten past two, which is fine now the pitch is paid for.
  • 09:15Brim the tank at Wasserbillig. Diesel at €1.89 against €2.16 in Germany and €2.46 at home. Fill right up, this tank has to last.
  • 11:20Stop for the dog and a coffee at the same exit. Find shade, not a bare forecourt.
  • 13:00Arrive at Camping Les Trois Châteaux. 10 rue du Bassin, on the western edge of Eguisheim. Reception only opens at two, so the first hour is shade and water for Fransje rather than a scramble to pitch up.
  • 14:00Check in and pitch up, then a ten-minute walk into Eguisheim for a late lunch in the shade of the arcaded streets.
  • 16:00Cave Wolfberger, 6 Grand Rue. Walk in, no appointment. Free tasting, dogs allowed inside, and a cool cellar at the hottest hour of the hottest day. The rosé and the Crémant are here.
  • 20:00Dinner in Eguisheim. Eat late, the squares only become pleasant after eight. You walk back to the van, which is the whole point of being parked here.
Sleep: Camping Les Trois Châteaux, two nights. Reception 14:00 to 19:00, gate open from seven.
Sat 15 Aug

Colmar and the Route du Vin

30 to 60 km locally

37°C again, and a national holiday. Colmar first thing while it is bearable and quiet, a long gap in the middle, then the tasting and a late dinner.

  • 08:30Colmar by camper. Petite Venise before the crowds and before the heat, then lunch in town. The Unterlinden opens at nine and is air-conditioned, which on this day is not a trivial argument.
  • 13:00Back before the worst of it. Then sit still. Do not plan anything between one and four.

  • 20:00Last evening in Eguisheim or Colmar. Busy, it being a holiday, so book something or eat late.
Get the awning packed dry tonight if you can. Sunday brings rain, and a wet awning rolled up is a job you inherit at home.
Sun 16 Aug

Eguisheim → Den Bosch

545 km · ±5 h 30

The heat breaks overnight: 30°C and rain, 7 mm of it. Cooler to drive in, but wet roads and the possibility of heavy showers after four days of building heat.

  • 08:30Break camp.
  • 09:15Depart. Via Strasbourg, Karlsruhe, Cologne. Toll-free the whole way.
  • 12:30Lunch stop and the last fuel, roughly halfway. In Germany and off the motorway: the ADAC measured a 43-cent gap between service stations and the village pump in exactly these two states. 25 litres is enough to get home.
  • 15:15Home.
Note: mid-August is when France swaps its holidaymakers over, the Sunday after the fifteenth is busy heading north, and it will be raining. Three good reasons to leave before ten rather than after.

What to watch

Saturday 15 August is a national holiday in France

Assumption Day. Supermarkets and shops are closed and villages are extra busy. Larger tourist-facing cellars like Wolfberger stay open, and Schoepfer-Muller have confirmed 16:00 in writing. It is retail that shuts, so do any stocking up on Friday.

Two days of 37°C, and the dog is the constraint

An open vineyard path at this temperature is genuinely dangerous for a dog: no shade, and stone and gravel that hold heat long after the air has cooled. Test the ground with the back of your hand before letting Fransje onto it. The vineyard walk up to the Steingrubler behind Wettolsheim is the one to skip this weekend.

Cellars run 12 to 14°C year round, which is why both tastings sit in the worst heat of the afternoon. Nights are 17 to 18°C, so the van cools down properly and sleeping is not the problem.

The two Alsace tastings

Friday 16:00 · 10 min on foot · walk in

Cave Wolfberger, Eguisheim

One of the largest cellars in Alsace, going since 1902, and the right thing for the day you arrive: no appointment, just turn up. Free entry, free tasting, dogs welcome inside, and a range from AOC Alsace through Grands Crus to Crémant and late harvest.

Not the most soulful tasting of the trip, but the widest choice at your price point, and it sits on the square you were eating on anyway.

Saturday 16:00 · 5 min · confirmed

Schoepfer-Muller, Wettolsheim

Same family since Louis Schoepfer arrived from Switzerland in 1656, run by Alexis Schoepfer since 2021, HVE3 certified. Crémant and Pinot Noir alongside the whites, so rosé and Crémant rosé both. Confirmed by mail for Saturday at four, the holiday notwithstanding.

On rosé in Alsace

Rosé here means Pinot Noir, the only red grape the appellation permits, either as a still rosé or as Crémant d'Alsace rosé. Both sit in the €8 to €14 range at cellar door. The Crémant is the more interesting buy of the two: Alsace makes more of it than anywhere in France outside Champagne, and at that price it is hard to beat.

The camper advantage

Two tastings, no driving

A tasting where you have to drive afterwards is a tasting where someone spits. Wolfberger is a ten-minute walk from the pitch, so Friday afternoon is a genuinely different afternoon. Saturday's five minutes to Wettolsheim is the only stretch where it matters, and it is the one worth staying sharp for.

This is what the Munster valley site would have cost you: every wine stop a fifteen to twenty minute drive each way.

The fuel plan, last leg

Wasserbillig on Friday morning is the proper fill at €1.89. After that there are still about 900 km to run and a 90-litre tank, so you come up roughly 80 km short of home: hence a 25-litre top-up on Sunday around lunchtime.

Never fill on the German Autobahn itself. The ADAC measured diesel at €2.61 a litre at motorway service stations in April 2026 against €2.28 just off the exit. In Rhineland-Palatinate and Baden-Württemberg, exactly where Sunday's route runs, the gap reaches 43 cents. Look for an Autohof rather than a Raststätte, or a village station just off the junction.

On top of that

Eating out, €200 to €350 for what is left: two dinners for two at €60 to €100, plus lunches and coffee. Entry and tastings, €30 to €50: the Unterlinden in Colmar, and Wolfberger is free while Schoepfer-Muller is likely credited against what you buy. Wine home, as much as you like — a case from the estate runs €90 to €250, and boot space is not the constraint.

If you want more

The Vosges, Saturday

The Route des Crêtes starting from Munster, 25 minutes from Eguisheim, is a beautiful mountain drive with views over the whole of Alsace, and several degrees cooler than the plain. Allow half a day and a fair number of hairpins. It only fits if you drop Colmar, so it is one or the other.

Haut-Kœnigsbourg

Forty minutes north of Colmar, the restored hilltop castle over the Rhine plain. A holiday-weekend crowd is guaranteed and it is a long way in the heat, so this is the one for a cooler return trip rather than this Saturday.
