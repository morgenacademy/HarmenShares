The trip in brief

Five days from Den Bosch to the Moselle, then south to the Alsatian wine villages, and home. Two bases and four nights: the campsite at Schweich, fifteen minutes from Roman Trier, and then Wettolsheim next to Colmar. The only heavy stretch is the drive home on Sunday, 5.5 hours, so that morning starts early.

Two nights on the Moselle turns Thursday into a proper wine day rather than a transfer day, and it hands the trip a second wine region for free. Riesling on the Moselle on Thursday, Alsace on Friday. Strasbourg becomes a three-hour stop on the way south instead of an overnight, which is the trade.

The Alsace tasting sits on Friday 14 August and not Saturday. Saturday is Assumption Day, a national holiday in France, when many wine estates and shops close. That leaves Saturday free for Colmar, Eguisheim and the Route des Vins, which stay open and lively.

It works with hotels or with a camper. The route and the timings are identical either way, only the overnight stops change. The camper version is set out further down.

What goes wrong if you do nothing

Saturday 15 August is a national holiday in France

Assumption Day. Supermarkets, shops and a fair share of wine estates are closed, while restaurants and villages are extra busy. That is why the serious tasting sits on Friday and not Saturday.

It does not write Saturday off, though. Larger tourist-facing cellars like Wolfberger in Eguisheim stay open on the holiday, and some small estates open precisely because it is the busiest day of the year. It is the family domaines you cannot assume anything about, which is why the Saturday plan has a fallback built into it.

Domaine Albert Mann is out: summer closure

Wettolsheim's best-known estate is closed from 30 July through 17 August. Exactly your week. Do not bother emailing, pick an alternative.

Strasbourg has a low-emission zone (ZFE)

You need a Crit'Air sticker to drive into the centre, Dutch plates included. It costs a few euros but delivery easily takes one to two weeks, so order it now rather than the week itself. Use the official French site only, plenty of resellers charge double.

Thursday: the walk

First choice · starts in Schweich

Rundweg Moselblick (R4)

9.2 km, about 2 h 45, 258 m of climb. Runs along the top edge of the vineyards with long views over the Moselle at Longuich, Kirsch and Riol, through the little wine hamlet of Longen, with a rest spot above the Longuich bridge. Easy to moderate, but wear proper shoes: a lot of it is loose vineyard track.

Starts from the campsite, so no driving at all.

Longer · starts in Longuich, 5 min

Moselsteig Seitensprung Longuicher Sauerbrunnen

12.6 km, about 3 h 20, 153 m of climb. More varied and more shaded: a Roman villa, the mineral spring it is named after, stretches of old woodland, then terraced vineyards and viewpoints. Circular, from the car park at the In den Kreuzfeldern roundabout.

Better choice if Thursday turns out hot.

Fransje can run here, with two caveats

Rhineland-Palatinate has no blanket leash law for ordinary forest and field. Dogs need to be under control, and that is the whole requirement. Nature reserves are leashed year round and some places impose seasonal rules during the breeding season, but that season is over well before mid-August, and neither of these routes crosses a reserve.

The two things to actually watch: the grapes are close to harvest in August, so keep to the paths and out of the rows, growers are understandably touchy about it. And there is game in the woodland stretches, which is the real reason to be able to call the dog back rather than just hope.

Thursday: Moselle wine

This is the region for the bottles you are missing

Estate-level Riesling from a serious Moselle producer sits at €9 to €14, Kabinett at €12 to €16, and cellar door is usually a euro or two under shop price. Grans-Fassian's Mineralschiefer Riesling trocken retails at €11.50, to give one concrete marker. Alsace at that price is decent village wine; the Moselle at that price is the real thing. It fills exactly the gap under your €20 to €25 shelf.

Thursday is the awkward day for small estates

Most Moselle growers keep their cellar door open Saturdays and take appointments the rest of the week. Bernkastel is the one address on this list that is reliably open on a Thursday afternoon without arranging anything. If you would rather have Leiwen, send a mail this week: a request for Thursday 13 August, mid-afternoon, two people, is almost always fine with notice.

Saturday: two more caveaux

This is the day the holiday bites, so ring ahead

15 August is Assumption Day and small family estates are a coin flip: some shut entirely, some open precisely because it is the busiest tourist day of the year. The two below are picked to cover both cases. Call Schoepfer-Muller now and ask about the fifteenth specifically, then you know well in advance whether Saturday needs a plan B.

How the three Alsace tastings divide up

Barmès-Buecher on Friday is the one to take seriously: biodynamic, small, by appointment, and the wines are worth the €18 to €35 they cost. That is the splurge.

Schoepfer-Muller and Wolfberger on Saturday are where the €7 to €14 bottles live, Wolfberger's Crémant especially. Between the Moselle on Thursday and these two on Saturday, the case that drives home should sit mostly in the bracket you were missing, with a few serious bottles on top.

If you take the camper instead

Same route, easier logistics

Nothing about the day-by-day plan changes. You drop three hotel bookings and pick up three overnight spots, all of which sit within walking or tram distance of what you came to see. Add roughly ten to fifteen percent to the driving times, and keep in mind that the Route des Vins villages have narrow streets, so park outside and walk in.

Worth setting up · All of France

France Passion

A membership scheme that lets you park overnight for free at participating farms and wine estates, and Alsace is well covered. No facilities, one night, no obligation to buy (though obviously you will). Order the guide well before you leave, it arrives by post.

The real camper advantage

A tasting where you have to drive afterwards is a tasting where someone spits. If you can park for the night at or near the estate, Friday afternoon becomes a genuinely different afternoon. Mention it when you email Barmès-Buecher, or line up a France Passion address nearby as the backup.

The Crit'Air sticker still applies

Campers are not exempt from Strasbourg's low-emission zone. And check your vehicle's Crit'Air class before you order: older diesel campers can fall into a category that is restricted rather than merely stickered. Worth five minutes now instead of a conversation at a checkpoint later.

What it costs

The camper is roughly €370 cheaper, and it is not the fuel that decides it

A Ducato on diesel drinks about €105 more over the 1,270 km than a petrol car does. But four nights of peak-August hotels cost around €480 more than two nights at Schweich and two at an Alsatian aire. The accommodation gap is nearly five times the fuel gap, so it swallows the difference whole.

Three things worth knowing about those numbers

This route is almost toll-free. Nothing charges you between Den Bosch and Trier, the A35 through Alsace is free, and coming home through Germany costs nothing either. Only the Trier to Strasbourg leg has a tolled option (roughly €10 to €17 via Metz on the A4) and a toll-free one through Germany that takes about twenty minutes longer. A camper over two metres tall counts as toll class 2 in France, around one and a half times the car rate, which on this route means almost nothing.

Filling up in Luxembourg is worth real money. Petrol there is about €1.76 against €2.40 in the Netherlands, diesel €1.89 against €2.46. One tank makes the difference: roughly €35 saved on a 55-litre car tank, €51 on a 90-litre camper tank. It is on the way on day one, so there is no reason not to.

The second Moselle night is nearly free. €26 in the camper, against a Strasbourg hotel night in mid-August at €150 to €220. Swapping the city night for a second night at Schweich pays for a good few bottles, and buys you the walk and the tasting on top.

Wine home

As much as you like

The honest budget line. A case of good Alsatian from the estate runs €90 to €250. The camper wins here too, since boot space stops being the constraint.

Where the estimate is softest

Accommodation. Mid-August is peak season in Strasbourg and Colmar, prices move a lot with how early you book, and the range above assumes a decent middle-bracket hotel rather than the cheapest room going. Everything else is reasonably firm: fuel prices are from late July 2026 and will drift a few cents, and the campsite rates come from the sites themselves.

Alternatives if you want less driving

Skip or shorten Strasbourg

If you would rather stay put longer, cut Strasbourg down to a long afternoon and evening and skip the overnight there. That gives you: Wednesday and Thursday on the Moselle, Friday afternoon in Strasbourg as a stop along the way, and three nights in Alsace. Same amount of driving, but you unpack twice instead of three times.

Or more: add the Vosges

On Saturday, the Route des Crêtes starting from Munster (20 minutes from Wettolsheim) is a beautiful mountain drive with views over the whole of Alsace. Allow half a day and a fair number of hairpins.
