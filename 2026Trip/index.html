The trip in brief

Five days from Den Bosch to the Moselle, then south to the Alsatian wine villages, and home. Two bases and four nights: the campsite at Schweich, fifteen minutes from Roman Trier, and then Eguisheim next to Colmar. The only heavy stretch is the drive home on Sunday, 5.5 hours, so that morning starts early.

Two nights on the Moselle turns Thursday into a proper wine day rather than a transfer day, and hands the trip a second wine region for free. Riesling on the Moselle on Thursday, Alsace on Friday and Saturday. Strasbourg is deliberately left out: on a 37-degree Friday, arriving in Alsace at one o'clock beats three hours of hot pavement.

The Alsace tasting sits on Friday 14 August and not Saturday. Saturday is Assumption Day, a national holiday in France, when many wine estates and shops close. That leaves Saturday free for Colmar, Eguisheim and the Route des Vins, which stay open and lively.

The trip is by camper, a Hymer Free 602 on a Fiat Ducato, 5.99 m long and 2.60 m tall, with a small dog along. Worth having to hand: that length clears the 6.50 m limit some sites impose, and the height puts you in French toll class 2. Both nights at Schweich are booked and confirmed, as are the Thursday and Saturday tastings. The overnight stops are listed further down.

The forecast

A heatwave, then rain on the way home

Four dry, cloudless days climbing from 33 to 37°C, then it breaks on Sunday. This is the single biggest factor in how the days below are timed, and it is what to pack for: heat all week, wet on the drive back.

What follows from it

Nights are fine. 14 to 18°C, so the camper cools down properly and sleeping is not the problem. It is the middle of each day that is.

The UV index is 6 all week and the sky is 90% clear. Skin burns fast at that level, and there is no cloud coming to help.

Fransje is the real constraint. At 35 to 37°C an open vineyard path is genuinely dangerous for a dog: no shade, and stone and gravel that hold heat long after the air has cooled. Hence the walk at half seven on Thursday morning on the wooded route, and no walk at all on Saturday. Test the ground with the back of your hand before letting her onto it.

Cellars are the coolest rooms in either region, sitting at 12 to 14°C year round. Both tastings are deliberately placed in the worst heat of the afternoon for exactly that reason.

Sunday is a different problem. After four days of building heat, the change comes with rain and possibly heavy showers. Get the awning packed dry on Saturday evening if you can, and reckon on wet roads rather than hot ones for the drive home.

What goes wrong if you do nothing

Saturday 15 August is a national holiday in France

Assumption Day. Supermarkets, shops and a fair share of wine estates are closed, while restaurants and villages are extra busy. That is why the serious tasting sits on Friday and not Saturday.

It does not write Saturday off, though. Larger tourist-facing cellars like Wolfberger stay open, and some small estates open precisely because it is the busiest day of the year. Schoepfer-Muller is one of them: they have confirmed 16:00 on the fifteenth in writing. Shops and supermarkets are the thing to plan around, so do any stocking up on Friday.

Domaine Albert Mann is out: summer closure

Wettolsheim's best-known estate is closed from 30 July through 17 August. Exactly your week. Do not bother emailing, pick an alternative.

Thursday: the walk

What actually happened: the Mosel bank, straight from the pitch. Both routes below start with a climb into open vineyard, and on a 35-degree morning that is the wrong half hour to spend. The towpath is flat, has trees in stretches and lets you turn round on the clock. The two loops stay here as the better option for a cooler day.
First choice · starts in Schweich

Rundweg Moselblick (R4)

9.2 km, about 2 h 45, 258 m of climb. Runs along the top edge of the vineyards with long views over the Moselle at Longuich, Kirsch and Riol, through the little wine hamlet of Longen, with a rest spot above the Longuich bridge. Easy to moderate, but wear proper shoes: a lot of it is loose vineyard track.

Starts from the campsite, so no driving at all.

Longer · starts in Longuich, 5 min

Moselsteig Seitensprung Longuicher Sauerbrunnen

12.6 km, about 3 h 20, 153 m of climb. More varied and more shaded: a Roman villa, the mineral spring it is named after, stretches of old woodland, then terraced vineyards and viewpoints. Circular, from the car park at the In den Kreuzfeldern roundabout.

Better choice if Thursday turns out hot.

Fransje can run here, with two caveats

Rhineland-Palatinate has no blanket leash law for ordinary forest and field. Dogs need to be under control, and that is the whole requirement. Nature reserves are leashed year round and some places impose seasonal rules during the breeding season, but that season is over well before mid-August, and neither of these routes crosses a reserve.

The two things to actually watch: the grapes are close to harvest in August, so keep to the paths and out of the rows, growers are understandably touchy about it. And there is game in the woodland stretches, which is the real reason to be able to call the dog back rather than just hope.

Thursday: Moselle wine

This is the region for the bottles you are missing

Estate-level Riesling from a serious Moselle producer sits at €9 to €14, Kabinett at €12 to €16, and cellar door is usually a euro or two under shop price. Grans-Fassian's Mineralschiefer Riesling trocken retails at €11.50, to give one concrete marker. Alsace at that price is decent village wine; the Moselle at that price is the real thing. It fills exactly the gap under your €20 to €25 shelf.

The Saturday question is settled

15 August is Assumption Day, and small family estates are normally a coin flip on that date. Schoepfer-Muller wrote back that 16:00 on the fifteenth suits them, so that is booked. What stays shut regardless is retail: do any shopping for food and supplies on Friday, not Saturday.

On rosé in Alsace

Rosé here means Pinot Noir, the only red grape the appellation permits, either as a still rosé or as Crémant d'Alsace rosé. Both sit in the €8 to €14 range at cellar door, so it lands squarely in the gap you were filling. The Crémant is the more interesting buy of the two: Alsace makes more of it than anywhere in France outside Champagne, and at that price it is hard to beat.

Three things worth knowing about those numbers

This route is almost toll-free. Nothing charges you between Den Bosch and Trier, the A35 through Alsace is free, and coming home through Germany costs nothing either. Only the leg south from Schweich has a tolled option (roughly €10 to €17 via Metz on the A4) and a toll-free one through Germany that takes about twenty minutes longer. A camper over two metres tall counts as toll class 2 in France, around one and a half times the car rate, which on this route means almost nothing.

The fuel plan is three stops, and only one of them is a proper fill. Dutch diesel at €2.46 is the most expensive on the whole route, so leave home with roughly 55 litres: enough for Wednesday, Thursday and the run to the Luxembourg border on Friday, and no more. Brim the tank at Wasserbillig on Friday morning at €1.89. Then a short top-up on Sunday around lunchtime, in Germany and off the motorway, before you cross back into the Netherlands.

Never fill on the German Autobahn itself. The ADAC measured diesel at €2.61 a litre at motorway service stations in April 2026 against €2.28 just off the exit, a gap of 33 cents. In Rhineland-Palatinate and Baden-Württemberg, exactly where Sunday's route runs, the gap reaches 43 cents. On a 40-litre top-up that is €13 to €17 for the sake of one exit and ten minutes. Look for an Autohof rather than a Raststätte, or a village station just off the junction.

Why not just fill once and be done. After Wasserbillig there are still about 900 km to run: 290 to Eguisheim, 60 locally on Saturday and 545 home. At 11 L/100 that is 98 litres against a 90-litre tank, so you come up roughly 80 km short of home. Hence the third stop, and 25 litres covers it.

The second Moselle night costs €26. That is the entire price of turning Thursday from a driving day into a walk and a tasting. It is comfortably the best-value decision in this whole plan.

Where the estimate is softest

Fuel, which is now most of the bill. The figures use late-July 2026 averages and pump prices move weekly, so treat the range as the range. Consumption is the other variable: 11 L/100 km is a fair average for a Ducato conversion at motorway speed, but a heavier build or a headwind pushes it to 12 or 13, which is another €30 or so. Alternatives if you want less driving

If the heat forecast eases

Should the week turn out five degrees cooler than currently predicted, Strasbourg goes back on the table as a three-hour stop on Friday: leave Schweich at 08:30, cathedral and Petite France and a winstub lunch, then on to Eguisheim by half three. With the pitch booked there is no longer a place to lose, so it comes down to the heat alone: only worth it if the forecast really does come down.

Or more: add the Vosges

On Saturday, the Route des Crêtes starting from Munster (25 minutes from Eguisheim) is a beautiful mountain drive with views over the whole of Alsace. Allow half a day and a fair number of hairpins.
